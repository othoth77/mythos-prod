## Important Notice

The Mouain Founding Pedagogical Council does NOT yet exist. This document defines its charter, composition, role, and activation criteria.

The council shall be formed BEFORE any Mouain implementation begins (see GATE-4 in `docs/MOUAIN_ROADMAP.md`).

---

## 1. Identity

### Official Name

**Mouain Founding Pedagogical Council**

**Arabic:** المجلس التربوي المؤسس لـ Mouain

### Positioning

The council is the pedagogical authority for Mouain. It does NOT write code. It co-designs and validates the pedagogical logic that Mouain will implement.

---

## 2. Role

The council's role is to co-design and validate:

### 2.1 Pedagogical Workflows

- How teachers actually prepare, teach, and assess
- Workflow differences between subjects
- Workflow differences between educational levels
- What AI assistance is valuable vs what is intrusive

### 2.2 Curriculum Structures

- How official curricula are organized
- How competencies, objectives, and activities relate
- How curriculum structure differs by subject
- What curriculum data Mouain needs to be useful

### 2.3 Teacher Workflows

- Daily, weekly, and unit-level planning patterns
- Assessment and correction workflows
- Communication patterns
- Administrative tasks that consume teaching time

### 2.4 Assessment Models

- How different subjects assess differently
- Formative vs summative assessment practices
- Rubric design and usage
- Competency-based assessment approaches

### 2.5 Educational AI Behaviour

- What AI assistance is pedagogically appropriate
- What AI should NOT do
- How AI output should be presented to teachers
- Confidence and transparency requirements

### 2.6 Modern Teaching Techniques

- Active learning approaches
- Differentiated instruction
- Project-based learning
- Digital and blended learning practices

### 2.7 Subject-Specific Requirements

- What makes Arabic teaching different from Mathematics
- What primary teachers need that secondary teachers do not
- Subject-specific tools, resources, and workflows

### 2.8 Classroom Usability

- Real classroom conditions and constraints
- Technology access realities
- Time pressure realities
- What works in practice vs what works in theory

---

## 3. What the Council Does NOT Do

- Write code
- Design user interfaces
- Make technology architecture decisions
- Manage the project
- Select AI models or platforms
- Define database schemas
- Make deployment decisions
- Handle business or financial decisions

---

## 4. Composition

### 4.1 Size

Minimum 3-5 educators. Maximum 8-10. Small enough to work effectively, diverse enough to represent different perspectives.

### 4.2 Required Diversity

The council should include educators representing:

- **Different educational levels:**
  - Primary education (at least one)
  - Preparatory education (at least one)
  - Secondary education (at least one)

- **Different subject domains:**
  - Languages (Arabic, French)
  - Mathematics
  - Sciences
  - Humanities/ Social Sciences

- **Different teaching contexts:**
  - Public school experience
  - Private school experience (if relevant)
  - Urban and rural teaching experience (valued)

### 4.3 Desired Qualities

- Active teaching practice (not retired or purely administrative)
- Interest in educational technology (not necessarily expertise)
- Willingness to co-design rather than consult
- Comfort with iterative, exploratory work
- Ability to communicate pedagogical reasoning to non-educators

### 4.4 Selection Process

Not defined at MOU-0. Selection will be:
- Based on pedagogical expertise, not technology expertise
- Transparent criteria
- Voluntary participation
- Compensated (model to be defined)

## 5. Working Method

### 5.1 Sessions

- Regular working sessions (frequency to be defined with council)
- Combination of individual reflection, group discussion, and co-design exercises
- Sessions structured around specific pedagogical questions, not open-ended brainstorming
- Documentation of all sessions for traceability

### 5.2 Outputs

- Validated pedagogical workflow descriptions
- Curriculum structure maps
- Subject-specific requirement documents
- AI behaviour specifications
- Quality criteria for AI-generated content
- MVP scope recommendations

### 5.3 Relationship with Technology Team

- Council defines pedagogical requirements
- Technology team assesses feasibility
- Council validates that implementations match pedagogical intent
- Iterative feedback loop: implement, review, refine

### 5.4 Decision Authority

- Council decisions on pedagogical matters are binding
- Technology team decisions on technical matters are binding
- Where they conflict, dialogue resolves — neither overrides the other's domain
- The teacher's pedagogical judgment is the highest authority

## 6. Council Activation

### 6.1 Activation Criteria (GATE-4)

The council is activated when:

1. Mouain identity and domain are confirmed (GATE-3)
2. At least 3 educators from different disciplines have confirmed participation
3. Initial working method and schedule are agreed
4. Charter is ratified by initial members
5. First session agenda is defined

### 6.2 Pre-Activation Preparation

Before the council first meets:

- Mouain vision and charter documents shared with prospective members
- Individual pre-reading and reflection time
- Technology team prepares questions (not answers)
- First session scope clearly bounded

### 6.3 First Session Scope

Suggested first session agenda:

1. Council charter review and ratification
2. Mouain vision presentation and discussion
3. "What would make this valuable for YOU?" — individual reflection
4. Teacher workflow mapping exercise (one subject as example)
5. Highest-cost task identification
6. AI boundaries discussion
7. Next session planning

## 7. Ethical Framework

### 7.1 Teacher Dignity

- Mouain respects the teaching profession
- No surveillance or performance monitoring without explicit consent
- AI is an assistant, not a supervisor
- Teachers are not data sources to be mined

### 7.2 Student Protection

- Student data is pedagogically necessary, not commercially valuable
- No student profiling without pedagogical purpose
- No student data shared externally without institutional consent
- Student privacy is architecturally protected, not policy-protected

### 7.3 Transparency

- Teachers know what AI is doing and why
- AI limitations are communicated clearly
- No hidden automation or decisions
- Council work is documented and traceable

### 7.4 Inclusivity

- Mouain serves diverse learners
- Accessibility is considered from the start
- Different teaching contexts (urban, rural, resource-constrained) are considered
- No feature that only works in privileged environments

---

## 8. Evolution

### 8.1 From Founding Council to Permanent Council

The Founding Council may evolve into a permanent Pedagogical Advisory structure as Mouain matures. Its role, composition, and working method will be reviewed at each major stage.

### 8.2 Specialist Sub-Councils

As Mouain grows, specialist groups may form for:
- Specific subjects (Arabic pedagogy group, Mathematics pedagogy group, etc.)
- Specific levels (primary pedagogy group, secondary pedagogy group)
- Specific workflows (assessment group, curriculum mapping group)

### 8.3 International Expansion

When Mouain expands to new countries/curricula, new pedagogical councils shall be formed for those contexts. Curriculum-specific pedagogical logic must come from curriculum-native educators.
