## Important Notice

This document records the **pedagogical model and design principles** for Mouain. These are foundational principles, not training data or implementation specifications.

Pedagogical design is owned by educators, not by technologists. This document defines the framework within which educators will design the actual pedagogical logic.

---

## 1. Pedagogical Philosophy

### 1.1 Teacher-Centred Design

Mouain is designed around the teacher's professional reality:

- The teacher is the pedagogical expert
- The teacher makes all pedagogical decisions
- Technology amplifies teacher capability, it does not substitute for it
- The system adapts to teaching style, not the reverse

### 1.2 Curriculum Fidelity

Mouain is anchored to official curricula, not to generic AI knowledge:

- Every learning activity is traceable to a curriculum objective
- AI-generated content is curriculum-aligned, not generic
- Curriculum interpretation is educator-validated, not AI-assumed
- Different curricula require different pedagogical logic

### 1.3 Subject and Level Differentiation

Different subjects and educational levels require fundamentally different pedagogical approaches:

- Arabic language teaching ≠ Mathematics teaching ≠ Science teaching
- Primary education ≠ Secondary education
- Pedagogy is not a prompt — it is structured professional knowledge

### 1.4 Progressive Competence

Mouain is designed around competency-based education principles:

- Learning is organized around competencies, not just topics
- Assessment is linked to competencies, not just grades
- Progress is tracked at the competency level
- Remediation targets specific competency gaps

## 2. Pedagogical Workflows

### 2.1 Lesson Planning

The teacher plans lessons aligned to curriculum objectives.

Mouain assists by:
- Presenting the relevant curriculum structure
- Suggesting activities aligned to learning objectives
- Generating structured lesson plans in teacher-preferred format
- Remembering teacher preferences across planning sessions

Mouain does NOT:
- Decide what to teach
- Choose pedagogical approach without teacher input
- Replace the teacher's professional judgment about pacing and sequencing

### 2.2 Exercise and Activity Design

The teacher creates or selects exercises.

Mouain assists by:
- Generating exercises aligned to specific learning objectives
- Varying exercise format (multiple choice, open-ended, problem-solving)
- Adjusting difficulty based on competency level
- Providing exercise variations for differentiated instruction

Mouain does NOT:
- Generate exercises without pedagogical justification
- Treat all subjects with identical exercise patterns
- Replace teacher-designed activities

### 2.3 Assessment

The teacher assesses student learning.

Mouain assists by:
- Building assessments with explicit competency links
- Generating rubrics aligned to learning objectives
- Organizing assessment data for analysis
- Identifying patterns across assessments

Mouain does NOT:
- Automatically grade without teacher review
- Make pass/fail decisions
- Reduce student performance to a single number
- Share assessment data without teacher/institutional control

### 2.4 Correction

The teacher corrects student work.

Mouain assists by:
- Pre-analyzing common error patterns
- Suggesting rubric application
- Organizing corrections for review
- Tracking correction status

Mouain does NOT:
- Automatically correct without teacher review
- Decide what constitutes a correct answer in open-ended work
- Replace teacher feedback with automated comments

### 2.5 Student Monitoring

The teacher monitors student progress.

Mouain assists by:
- Tracking competency progression over time
- Identifying recurring difficulties
- Generating progress summaries
- Highlighting students who may need additional support

Mouain does NOT:
- Profile students without pedagogical purpose
- Share progress data without authorization
- Make decisions about student placement or intervention

## 3. Subject-Specific Considerations

### 3.1 Arabic Language

- Grammar and morphology are highly structured (nahw, sarf)
- Text comprehension requires cultural and linguistic context
- Writing instruction follows specific pedagogical traditions
- Calligraphy and dictation are distinct skills
- Literary analysis requires curriculum-specific frameworks

### 3.2 French Language (as Foreign/Second Language)

- Different from native French instruction
- Specific Tunisian curriculum approach
- Oral and written competencies are separately assessed
- Cultural context differs from metropolitan French

### 3.3 Mathematics

- Highly sequential — prerequisite tracking is critical
- Multiple solution paths must be recognized
- Visual representations (geometry, graphs) essential
- Procedural fluency vs conceptual understanding distinction
- Problem-solving requires domain-specific scaffolding

### 3.4 Sciences

- Experimental and theoretical components
- Safety considerations for practical work
- Observation, hypothesis, experimentation cycle
- Subject-specific vocabulary in both Arabic and French

### 3.5 History, Geography, Civics

- Culturally specific content
- Arabic and French content sources
- Document analysis skills
- Chronological and spatial reasoning

### 3.6 Islamic Education (where applicable)

- Specific pedagogical traditions
- Sensitive handling of religious content
- Curriculum-aligned scope

### 3.7 Physical Education

- Practical, not digital-native
- Assessment based on physical competencies
- Health and safety considerations

## 4. Educational Levels

### 4.1 Primary Education (المرحلة الابتدائية)

- Foundational literacy and numeracy
- Single-teacher or few-teacher model
- Integrated subjects in early years
- High importance of concrete/manipulative learning
- Parent communication is frequent
- Assessment is primarily formative

### 4.2 Preparatory Education (المرحلة الإعدادية)

- Subject specialization begins
- Multiple teachers per student
- Transition from concrete to abstract thinking
- Formal examinations introduced
- Discipline-specific pedagogical approaches emerge

### 4.3 Secondary Education (المرحلة الثانوية)

- High subject specialization
- Baccalaureate preparation pressure
- Intensive curriculum coverage
- Complex assessment and grading
- Section differentiation (Sciences, Letters, Mathematics, etc.)

## 5. Pedagogical Quality Principles

### 5.1 AI-Generated Content Quality

- All AI-generated pedagogical content must be reviewable
- Content must cite curriculum references
- Confidence indicators for AI suggestions
- Teacher override always available

### 5.2 Differentiation

- Same curriculum objective, different approaches for different students
- Not "easier" content but different representations and scaffolds
- Teacher decides when and how to differentiate

### 5.3 Inclusion

- Design for diverse learning needs
- Accessibility is not optional
- Multiple representation modes (text, visual, audio)

### 5.4 Language

- Arabic-first for Tunisian context
- French for subjects taught in French
- Clear separation of language domains
- No AI-generated mixed-language content without pedagogical purpose

---

## 6. What Mouain Does NOT Do (Pedagogically)

- Replace the teacher as the pedagogical authority
- Make decisions about student progression
- Automate grading without teacher review
- Recommend actions that bypass teacher judgment
- Profile students in ways teachers cannot explain
- Generate content that cannot be traced to curriculum objectives
- Apply identical pedagogical logic across different subjects
- Substitute for professional teacher training
- Operate without educator oversight

---

## 7. Validation Framework

Before any Mouain implementation is deployed to real teachers:

1. **Pedagogical Council review** — every major pedagogical feature
2. **Teacher testing** — real teachers in real preparation contexts
3. **Curriculum alignment audit** — independent verification
4. **Subject specialist sign-off** — per subject, per level

Technology validation (correctness, performance) is separate from pedagogical validation (appropriateness, quality).
